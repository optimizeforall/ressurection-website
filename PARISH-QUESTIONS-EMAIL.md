**Subject:** A few questions about the church, mostly one-line answers

Dear Fr. Ioann, [and copy to whoever looks after the website, if that is someone else]

I hope this finds you well. The new website is close to finished, and in going through it as though I had never been to the church, I found a handful of places where a visitor is left without an answer that only you can give.

Almost all of these need one line back. **"We don't do that" is a genuinely useful answer** — it means the site can say so plainly instead of leaving a blank, which is always better for the person reading. Please answer whichever you can, in any order, in a quick reply, or by telephone if that is easier. My number is [your telephone number] and I am glad to take these over the phone and write them up myself.

**If you only have time for two things**, please answer the first group and the questions about the building. Those two matter more than everything else here put together.

## 1. Someone who needs a priest urgently

This is the one that concerns me most. Right now the Contact page tells people to call about baptisms, weddings and memorials — which is right for someone planning ahead, but says nothing to a person whose father is dying tonight, or who needs to confess before morning. Someone may well come to the website at the worst moment of their life, and at the moment it does not help them.

- Which number should they call in an emergency, and is it answered after hours or does it go to a machine?
- Is there anyone else to try if you cannot be reached?
- What should they expect — should they leave a message with their name and where they are, and you will call back?
- Do you go out to hospitals, care homes and private houses for confession, Communion or Unction? How far, and how should someone ask?
- If a family has a death and needs an Orthodox funeral, what is the first thing they should do — call you, or the funeral home? Is there a cemetery or funeral home locally that the parish usually works with?

## 2. Someone deciding whether they can physically come

The site tells a visitor where to park and nothing else about the building. An elderly person, someone using a wheelchair or a walker, or a parent with a small child cannot plan a visit without these facts — and most of them will not telephone to ask. They will simply not come.

- Are there steps at the entrance, and how many? Is there a handrail, or a level or ramped way in at any door?
- Can a wheelchair or walker get into the church?
- Is there anywhere to park close to the door, or to pull up and let someone out?
- Where are the restrooms — upstairs by the church, or downstairs? Is the meal afterwards downstairs, and is there any way down besides the stairs?
- Is there seating for people who cannot stand for a long service?

## 3. Someone who wants to take part

The site shows a full parish life in the photographs but never explains how a person joins any of it.

- Who should someone speak to about singing in the choir, reading, serving in the altar, helping with the meal, or joining a cleaning or work day? Is there a sisterhood?
- Is there a church school for children, anything for teenagers, or a class for inquirers? A plain no is perfectly good — we would then say "speak with Fr. Ioann and he will meet with you", which reads much better than silence.
- The website mentions the skete and the daily prayer of the brotherhood, but says nothing about visiting. Who is there now, may a visitor come to a weekday service, can a pilgrim stay, and how should they ask?

## 4. Four smaller practical things

- Are molebens served on request, and how does someone ask for one?
- How should names be given for commemoration — a note handed to you before the Liturgy, or some other way? Is there a customary offering, or should we say there is no set amount?
- If an Orthodox visitor from another parish or jurisdiction wants to commune, do you expect a word from their priest, or is it enough to come at 9:00 for confession?
- Cheques: the giving page currently says to make them out to "Church of the Resurrection of Christ Inc", and elsewhere the full name of the church is used. Which exact name should a donor write?

Nothing here is urgent except the first group, and none of it needs to arrive all at once. Even a few sentences on the emergency contact and the steps at the door would let me finish the two pages that matter most.

With thanks for your time, and asking your blessing,

[your name]

# What we need from the parish

This document lists everything the new website still needs from the Russian Orthodox Church and Skete of the Resurrection of Christ before it can go live, plus a few recommendations we would like to offer.

The site itself is built and working. What follows is not a list of problems with the site — it is a list of facts only the parish can confirm, material only the parish can supply, and decisions only the parish can make.

**How to use this document.** Items written as `- [ ]` are things we need back from you. You can tick them off as you go, or simply reply to whichever ones you can answer. Nothing has to arrive all at once, but **Section A must be settled before the site goes public**, because those items are either wrong-if-guessed or legally significant.

Everything on the site was drawn from the current site at resurrectionskete.org, from photographs the parish supplied, and from public diocesan information. Where we were not certain, we have said so below rather than inventing something.

## B. Content we need from you

Nothing here blocks launch, but each item makes the site substantially more truthful and more useful.

### B1. Parish and skete history

The About page is currently written in general terms, because **we had no history to work from** — the old site never told the story of this community. What is there now is accurate but written from the outside, and it shows.

A page of roughly 400–800 words in the rector's own words would replace it entirely. Please cover whatever you can:

- [ ] When the parish was **founded**, and by whom
- [ ] When the **church building** was built or acquired, and how
- [ ] How the **skete** began — whose idea it was, when the brotherhood was established, how a monastic community and a parish came to share one building
- [ ] Who **built or wrote the iconostasis**, and when. (Iconostases are usually the work of a named iconographer or workshop, and that person deserves the credit.)
- [ ] Roughly how large the community is — a range is fine ("a few dozen on a typical Sunday")
- [ ] Anything distinctive a first-time visitor should know
- [ ] The same in Russian, if someone can write it

### B2. The monastic life of the skete

A monastic community attached to a parish is unusual in Minnesota, and it is part of why people seek this church out. The site says the skete exists but cannot say what life there is like.

- [ ] A paragraph or two: **who lives at the skete**, how many, under what rule
- [ ] The **daily cycle** — what services the brotherhood keeps that are not on the public schedule, and whether visitors may attend any of them
- [ ] Any work, craft, or ministry of the brotherhood that could be described
- [ ] Whether visitors or pilgrims can stay, and how someone would ask

### B3. The schedule of services for the coming months

This is the item that most affects whether the site feels alive. A church website that is weeks out of date reads as abandoned.

The site reads its schedule from a small file, `data/events.json`, which a volunteer can edit. It currently holds 14 entries.

- [ ] The schedule of services for the **next two or three months** — feast days, weekday Liturgies, anything outside the normal weekly pattern
- [ ] The **Holy Week and Pascha schedule** as soon as it is set. This is the highest-traffic week of the year for any Orthodox parish website.
- [ ] Whichever form is easiest for you: a simple typed list of dates and times, a monthly calendar, a photograph of the printed calendar, or the bulletin
- [ ] **Who will keep this updated going forward**, and how would they prefer to do it — email a list to the site maintainer, or edit the file themselves?

### B4. The weekly bulletin

- [ ] **Who produces the bulletin** each week?
- [ ] Can they send each new issue to whoever maintains the site — by email, or by dropping it in a shared folder (Google Drive, Dropbox)?
- [ ] Should the **full archive** stay public (464 issues going back to 2014), or would you rather show only the last year or two?

### B5. Photographs

We currently have **three recent photographs** — the exterior in winter, the nave with the iconostasis, and the chapel with stained glass — plus the archive copied from the old site. **The newest album in that archive is from 2021**, and most of it stops around 2015. The gallery therefore reads as history rather than as a living parish.

Recent, high-resolution photographs would change the feel of the entire site more than any other single contribution.

- [ ] The **church exterior**, in more than one season if possible
- [ ] The **interior** — the nave, the sanctuary, the choir
- [ ] The **iconostasis**, photographed straight on and in good light
- [ ] **Parish life** — coffee hour, the meal after Liturgy, feast days, the church school, work days
- [ ] Anything from **2022 to today**: Pascha, Nativity, St. Seraphim day, ordinations, visits by the bishop

A dump of photographs straight from a phone or a Google Drive folder is genuinely more valuable to us than carefully selected ones. We can crop, resize, and choose.

### B6. Captions for the archive

Many of the 53 albums carry only a bare title — "Pascha 2009", "Nativity 2011" — and the individual photographs have no captions at all.

- [ ] For any albums where someone remembers: **what the occasion was**, and **who is pictured** (where those people would be happy to be named)
- [ ] Any album titles that are simply wrong or mis-dated
- [ ] This does not need to be exhaustive. Even a dozen well-captioned photographs of significant events are worth more than 600 uncaptioned ones.

### B7. Photographs that should be REMOVED — please review for privacy

**This needs a decision from the parish, and we would rather you make it than us.** The archive we copied from the old site contains 647 photographs spanning roughly twenty years. It includes material that many parishes today would not put on a public website:

- **Funerals and burials**, including open-casket photographs
- **Baptisms**, including infants and adults in baptismal garments
- **Weddings**, which are private family occasions
- **Children**, many of whom are now adults who never consented to being online and may not wish to be
- Photographs of people who have since **left the parish**, or who have **reposed**

These were all on the old public site, so nothing here is newly exposed. But a rebuild is the natural moment to ask the question.

- [ ] Please have someone who knows the community **review the gallery** and tell us which albums or individual photographs should come down.
- [ ] As a starting suggestion, consider removing **funeral photographs** and **photographs of identifiable children** unless a parent has agreed.
- [ ] Tell us if you would rather we **remove an entire category** (all funerals, all baptisms) rather than reviewing photo by photo — that is a perfectly reasonable choice and much faster.

We will remove anything you name, no questions asked.

### B8. Homilies and readings — now hosted in full, and translated

On your instruction the readings are no longer links to the old site: every one is now a page here, and **every one appears in both English and Russian**. Each has its own address matching the old site's, so `resurrectionskete.org/thomas.html` becomes `thomas.html` here and existing links keep working.

What we found when we moved them:

- **48 pages** existed on the old site. **Two were empty** — `prepconfsecondcomm` ("Preparation for Confession – 2nd Commandment") has no text at all, and `prophjohn` ("Святой Великий Пророк и Предтеча Иоанн") is a title and an icon with nothing beneath it. They are not on the new site.
  - [ ] Do you have the **missing text** for these two? The second-commandment page in particular looks like it was meant to complete a series — the third and fourth commandments are there.
- **Three pages were bulletin inserts carrying two unrelated articles at once**, an English one beside a Russian one. We split each into two separate readings, since they were never translations of one another:
  - "Mother's Day" (English) was sharing a page with the life of **St Ignatius Brianchaninov** (Russian)
  - the life of **St John the Theologian** (English) was sharing a page with the poem **«Пропущенная вечерня»** (Russian)
  - "The Dormition Fast" (English) was sharing a page with the **Smolensk Icon "Hodigitria"** (Russian)
- That leaves **49 readings**, about 27,000 words, each now in both languages.

**Every translation was made for this website and none has been checked by a person.** Each translated text carries a visible note saying so. They read well and the church vocabulary is right, but a translation of a homily is a matter of voice as much as accuracy.

- [ ] Could someone in the parish **read through the translations**, even a few at a time? The note comes off each page as it is approved. If you tell us which are approved we will clear the notes.
- [ ] Are there **newer homilies** from recent years that were never posted?
**On how the readings are now set.** Most of these pieces arrived as a handful of enormous blocks of text — "The Universal Exaltation of the Cross" was 619 words without a single break — which is punishing to read on a telephone, where most people will meet them. The pages now do the following, and **not one word of anybody's text has been added, removed or reworded**:

- **Long blocks are broken into paragraphs**, always at the end of a sentence, never in the middle of one. A prayer or a poem is left as one piece.
- **Section headings are set as headings.** Where "Prosphora" or "Kissing the Cross" was just another line of text, it is now a heading with a rule under it, so a reader can find their place.
- **The occasion and the appointed Gospel and Epistle** ("Second Sunday, All the Saints who have shone forth in the land of Russia / Mt. 4:18-23 / Rom. 2:10-16") are set apart above the sermon rather than reading as its first sentences.
- **Capitals are quieted.** The few passages typed in full capitals are now set in ordinary case; where they were term labels — the four ranks of penitents, "the Weepers", "the Hearers" — they are set in bold instead of shouted.
- **Typewriter marks are repaired**: `` ``quotes'' `` become proper quotation marks, `--` becomes a dash, and eighteen places where two sentences ran together without a space have been separated.

A script checks every page against the original scrape word by word, so this cannot quietly lose a sentence.

- [ ] Have a look at one or two and tell us if the paragraphing feels right. It is easily made looser or tighter across all 49 at once.

A few source quirks turned up while translating. We did not silently "correct" an author's text, except where it was clearly a scrape artifact rather than part of the reading:

- [ ] **Theophany (`bogoyavleniye`)** had a Nativity thank-you from Fr. Ioann pasted onto the last sentence of the homily, with no space. We took it off the page. It is a parish notice, not part of the sermon.
- [ ] **Radonitsa** cites "neither life nor death" as Romans 8:30 (it is 8:38-39) and "love will cover a multitude of sins" as 1 Peter 4:18 (it is 4:8). The numbers are as they stood on the old site.
- [ ] **On Orthodoxy** (St John of Shanghai) labels "Not everyone that saith unto Me, Lord, Lord" as Matt. VII:5 (it is 7:21) and "as ye have heard that Antichrist shall come" as I John II:19 (it is 2:18). Same policy.
- [ ] **St Ignatius Brianchaninov** says he retired to the Nikolo-Babaevsky monastery in 1871 and died in 1867. The received date of his death is 1867; the retirement was 1861. Left as found.
- [ ] Two articles on the old site shared the title "How We Should Conduct Ourselves in Church". The index now distinguishes them: one is *Entering and Standing*, the other *Communion and After*.

The choir recording of *Да исправится* is still on the Readings index.

## C. Rights and permissions

We have been deliberately conservative here. Where we could not establish that the parish owns something or has permission to publish it, we left it off the new site rather than copying it across. Each item below is a decision for the parish, and in a couple of cases may be worth a brief conversation with the copyright holder.

- [ ] **Fr Andrew Phillips is alive and still writing**, and four readings are his. His *Orthodox England* site is his own publication. A short note to him would settle the matter comfortably, and he would most likely be glad the pieces are being read.

None of this is urgent, and nothing here is unusual for a parish site that grew over twenty years. But it is the sort of thing that is much easier to tidy now than after a complaint.

### D4. Who maintains the site?

This is the question that determines the shape of everything else.

- [ ] **Who will look after the site week to week?** A parishioner, the rector, a volunteer, a paid service?
- [ ] **How comfortable are they with editing files?** Please answer honestly — there is no wrong answer, and getting this right saves a great deal of frustration later.

The site is currently plain HTML files. That is fast, free to host, and will still work in fifteen years, but updating it means editing a text file. If the person maintaining it would rather click buttons in a browser, we should add a small content management system before launch rather than after. Roughly:

- **Comfortable editing a text file** → stay with plain HTML. Nothing more to do.
- **Comfortable with a spreadsheet but not code** → keep plain HTML, but we set up the schedule and bulletins so they can be edited in a simple form.
- **Wants to click buttons in a browser** → add a lightweight CMS (Decap, Sanity, or similar). This is a real decision with a real cost, and it is much cheaper to make now.

### D5. Email address

The parish email is currently `rusmnch@msn.com`. It works, but an MSN address looks dated to a first-time visitor and is tied to a personal account rather than to the parish.

- [ ] Consider moving to a **domain address** such as `info@resurrectionskete.org` or `office@resurrectionskete.org`. This usually costs nothing beyond the domain and can simply forward to the existing MSN inbox, so nobody has to change how they read their mail.
- [ ] Tell us which address should appear on the site.
- [ ] If the parish keeps `rusmnch@msn.com`, that is fine — we just want it to be a decision rather than an accident.

### D6. The contact form

The Contact page currently offers a **`mailto:` link** — clicking it opens the visitor's own email program. This is simple and has no running costs, but it fails for anyone using webmail on a computer where no mail program is configured, which is a meaningful share of visitors.

- [ ] Would you like a **real contact form** instead? This needs a small backend service — **Formspree** or **Netlify Forms** are the usual choices, both have free tiers adequate for a parish, and both simply email submissions to whatever address you give them.
- [ ] If a form is added, **who receives the messages**, and who is responsible for answering them?
- [ ] If nobody can commit to answering a form promptly, **keeping the `mailto:` link is the better choice** — an unanswered contact form is worse than no form.

## E. Recommendations

These are suggestions, not work already done and not things you must accept. Each is offered with a reason.

**1. Replace the background image with a photograph of your own church.**
The site currently uses a generic sky-and-domes background — a stock photograph of a Russian church that is not yours. Everything else on this site is genuinely from and about this parish, and the background quietly undercuts that. A wide photograph of your own building, or a detail of your own iconostasis, would be better in every respect: more honest, more distinctive, and free of the provenance question raised in Section C4. This is the single highest-impact visual change available, and it needs only one good photograph.

**2. Offer a live stream, or at least recordings.**
Many parishes began streaming during the pandemic and quietly continued, because it serves the housebound, the ill, the elderly, and parishioners travelling. If you already stream, we should link it prominently from the homepage. If you do not, even posting an occasional recording of the choir or a homily gives distant enquirers a sense of what your services are actually like — which is exactly what someone considering a first visit wants to know.

**3. Use a liturgical calendar feed instead of hand-editing dates.**
The old site offered iCal and Google Calendar links. If the parish keeps its schedule in a Google Calendar, the website can read from it directly, meaning the schedule updates itself the moment someone changes the calendar — and parishioners can subscribe to it on their phones. This removes the most common way a church website goes stale, and it replaces a recurring chore with a one-time setup.

**4. Add recurring giving, not just one-off gifts.**
People who love a small parish often prefer to give $25 every month than $100 once. Recurring gifts are also far easier to budget against than sporadic ones. Whatever platform you settle on in Section A7, ask for a recurring option alongside the single gift, and consider naming one concrete need next to it.

**5. Have a native Russian speaker proofread the translations.**
Every page has an English/Russian toggle, and the Russian is a genuine translation rather than machine output — but **it was written by the developer, not by a native speaker**, and it has not been reviewed by anyone in the parish. Please have Fr. Ioann or a parishioner who writes Russian well read through it and mark corrections. The register matters as much as the accuracy: Church Slavonic forms where they belong, ordinary modern Russian everywhere else. This is not optional polish — a website is judged by the language its readers speak best.

**6. Caption the photographs that matter.**
The site is keyboard-navigable, has skip links, and every image has alternative text for screen readers. But the alt text on the **647 archive photographs is generic** ("Pascha 2009 — 14"), which is honest but not useful to a blind visitor. Rather than captioning 647 photographs, pick the thirty or forty that matter — the church building, the iconostasis, ordinations, the bishop's visits — and describe those properly. Anyone using a screen reader gets far more from a few good descriptions than from hundreds of numbers.

**7. Adopt a simple photo release policy.**
Section B7 asks you to review twenty years of photographs after the fact, which is tedious. A short written policy going forward avoids repeating that work: a note in the bulletin that photographs are taken at parish events and may appear on the website, a person to tell if you would rather not be photographed, and a rule about children (usually: not without a parent's word). One paragraph, agreed once, and the question is settled for good.

**8. Start a small email list.**
For people who already know you, a monthly note — this week's bulletin, the feasts coming up, one photograph — does more than a website ever will. Mailchimp, Buttondown, or even a Google Group would serve. We have not built this because it needs a parish decision and, more importantly, somebody willing to write the letters.

**9. Keep the photo originals out of the deployed site.**
The archive is **127 MB of full-size originals** in `assets/gallery/`, and the site does not use them — it serves only the optimised copies in `assets/gallery-web/` (80 MB). We have added `assets/gallery/` to `.gitignore` so the originals stay on disk but do not bloat the repository or the deployment. **The originals should be preserved somewhere safe** — an external drive and a cloud backup — because they are the parish's archive and the web copies are compressed. This is easily reversed if you would rather keep everything together.

There are also six small unreferenced files in `assets/` (`image1.webp`, `image2.webp`, `image3.jpg` — which are duplicates of the three recent parish photographs under meaningless names — plus `bg-header.jpg`, `parish-photo.jpg`, and `metropolitan-philaret.jpg`). Together they come to about half a megabyte, so they are harmless. We have left them in place rather than deleting parish material without asking, but they can be removed whenever you confirm they are not wanted.

**10. Review the site once a year.**
The old site still listed **Archbishop Peter, who reposed in November 2024**. That is not a criticism of anyone; it is simply what happens to every website without a scheduled review. Put a recurring reminder in the parish calendar — the patronal feast is a natural date — to check the clergy list, the hierarchs' names, the service times, and the phone number. Fifteen minutes once a year prevents the single most common way a parish website becomes misleading.
